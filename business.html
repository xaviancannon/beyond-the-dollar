THE FUNDAMENTALS

What Is Business?

A business is an organization that provides goods or services to customers. Businesses combine resources, people, ideas, and capital to create value.

Some businesses operate locally, while others become multinational corporations serving customers around the world.

💰

Generate Revenue

Businesses earn money by selling products, providing services, or using other revenue models.

🎯

Create Value

Successful businesses offer something customers consider useful, valuable, or worth purchasing.

📈

Grow and Expand

Companies can expand into new markets, develop products, and reach additional customers.

01 / FINANCIAL FUNDAMENTALS

How Businesses Make Money

Understanding the financial side of a business is essential. Revenue, expenses, and profit help explain whether a company is financially successful.

Revenue

Revenue is the total amount of money a business receives from selling goods or services before subtracting expenses.

Example

A company sells 500 products for $20 each. Its revenue is $10,000.

Expenses

Expenses are the costs a business pays to operate. These can include rent, employee wages, materials, advertising, and utilities.

Example

A company spends $6,000 on operating costs during a month.

Profit

Profit is the amount remaining after a business subtracts its expenses from its revenue.

Formula

Profit = Revenue − Expenses

Loss

A business experiences a loss when its expenses exceed its revenue during a given period.

Example

Revenue of $5,000 and expenses of $6,000 result in a $1,000 loss.

Revenue is not the same as profit.

A company can generate millions of dollars in revenue and still lose money if its expenses are even higher. This is why looking at profit is important when evaluating a business.

02 / REVENUE STRATEGIES

Business Models

A business model explains how a company creates value for customers and generates revenue. Different companies can use very different approaches.

🛍️

Product Sales

Companies sell physical or digital products directly to customers.

Example: Clothing retailers and electronics stores.

🔁

Subscriptions

Customers pay recurring fees, usually monthly or annually, to access a product or service.

Example: Streaming and software services.

📢

Advertising

Companies earn revenue by allowing advertisers to reach their audiences.

Example: Advertising-supported media platforms.

🤝

Transaction Fees

Companies collect fees when customers or businesses complete transactions through them.

Example: Payment processors and marketplaces.

📜

Licensing

A company allows another organization to use its intellectual property in exchange for payment.

Example: Software licenses and brand licensing.

🧰

Service-Based

Businesses charge customers for performing work or providing specialized expertise.

Example: Consulting and accounting firms.

03 / STARTING A BUSINESS

Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying opportunities and creating or developing businesses to pursue them.

Turning an Idea Into a Business

Entrepreneurs identify problems, research potential customers, develop solutions, and organize the resources needed to bring an idea to market.

Starting a business involves uncertainty. Entrepreneurs must consider startup costs, competition, customer demand, and the possibility that their idea may not succeed.

🚀
01

Identify an Opportunity

Find a problem, unmet need, or market opportunity that could support a business.

02

Research the Market

Study potential customers, competitors, demand, and possible pricing.

03

Develop a Business Plan

Outline the product, revenue model, costs, target customers, and strategy.

04

Launch and Improve

Introduce the business, learn from customer feedback, and adjust as necessary.

04 / REACHING CUSTOMERS

Marketing and Branding

Marketing involves understanding customer needs and communicating why a product or service is valuable. Branding helps establish how customers recognize and perceive a company.

Target Market

A target market is the specific group of customers a business aims to reach.

Companies research customer interests, purchasing behavior, and needs to identify the audiences most likely to buy their products.

Brand Identity

Brand identity includes a company's name, logo, visual design, messaging, and overall personality.

A recognizable brand can help a business distinguish itself from competitors.

Digital Marketing

Businesses use websites, search engines, email, and social media to reach potential customers.

Digital marketing can help companies reach audiences across geographic boundaries.

Customer Loyalty

Customer loyalty develops when people repeatedly choose a business over alternatives.

Product quality, customer service, trust, and consistent experiences can contribute to customer loyalty.

05 / THE MARKETPLACE

Competition and Business Strategy

Businesses compete for customers, resources, employees, and market share. Their strategies influence how they position themselves and respond to changes in the marketplace.

Why Competition Matters

Competition can encourage businesses to improve products, offer better customer experiences, develop new technologies, and reconsider their pricing.

A company must understand what makes it different and why customers might choose it instead of an alternative.

🏆

Cost Leadership

A company attempts to operate efficiently and offer competitive prices through careful cost management.

Differentiation

A company distinguishes its products or services through features, quality, design, service, or brand identity.

Market Expansion

A company seeks new customers by entering additional geographic markets or reaching different customer groups.

KEY TERMINOLOGY

Business Vocabulary

Understanding common business terminology makes it easier to analyze companies and their financial performance.

Capital

Money or other resources used to start, operate, or expand a business.

Market Share

The percentage of total sales in a market captured by a particular company.

Cash Flow

The movement of money into and out of a business over a period of time.

Startup

A newly established business, often created to develop a product or pursue an opportunity.

Assets

Resources owned or controlled by a company that have economic value.

Liabilities

Financial obligations a company owes to other parties.

Equity

The ownership interest remaining after liabilities are subtracted from assets.

Scalability

The ability of a business to grow without costs increasing at the same rate.

KEEP EXPLORING

Business Is Part of a Bigger Picture

Companies operate within larger economic systems. Interest rates, inflation, consumer demand, international trade, and government policies can all influence business decisions.
