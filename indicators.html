Understanding the economy

Economic Indicators

How do economists know whether an economy is growing? What causes prices to rise? What do interest rates reveal about financial conditions?

Economic indicators help answer these questions by measuring important parts of an economy.

Explore the indicators Explore real data

01 / The fundamentals

What are economic indicators?

Economic indicators are statistics that help describe the condition and direction of an economy. They can measure production, prices, employment, borrowing costs, and activity in financial markets.

Governments, businesses, economists, and investors use these measurements to evaluate economic conditions and make decisions.

📈

Economic growth

Measures whether an economy is producing more goods and services over time.

🛒

Price stability

Tracks changes in the prices consumers and businesses pay.

👥

Employment

Helps show how many people are working and how many are looking for jobs.

02 / Economic growth

Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

Gross Domestic Product, commonly called GDP, measures the market value of final goods and services produced within a country's borders during a particular period.

What does GDP measure?

GDP provides a broad measurement of economic production. When real GDP increases, it generally means an economy is producing more goods and services than before.

Nominal GDP vs. Real GDP

Nominal GDP measures production using current prices. Real GDP adjusts for changes in the overall price level, making it more useful for comparing economic production over time.

Why GDP matters

Economists use GDP to evaluate economic growth, compare economic activity across periods, and understand the overall size of an economy. However, GDP does not measure every aspect of people's well-being, income distribution, or quality of life.

03 / Prices and purchasing power

Inflation

Inflation is a general increase in the prices of goods and services over time. When inflation occurs, money generally loses purchasing power because the same amount of money buys fewer goods and services.

Consumer Price Index (CPI)

The Consumer Price Index measures changes over time in the prices paid by consumers for a representative group of goods and services.

It is one of the most widely followed measures of consumer inflation in the United States.

What causes inflation?

Inflation can result from stronger demand, higher production costs, supply disruptions, or a combination of economic factors.

Different causes can require different economic responses.

Inflation is not the same as prices falling

If inflation slows, prices are generally still increasing, but at a slower rate. A sustained decline in the overall price level is called deflation.

04 / The labor market

Unemployment

The unemployment rate measures the percentage of people in the labor force who do not have a job, are available to work, and are actively looking for employment.

Understanding the labor force

The labor force includes people who are employed and people who are unemployed but actively seeking work.

People who are not working and are not actively looking for a job are generally not counted as unemployed in the official unemployment rate.

Why unemployment matters

Employment conditions influence household income, consumer spending, business activity, and government tax revenue.

Economists also examine measures beyond the headline unemployment rate to understand the labor market more completely.

Important distinction

A low unemployment rate can indicate a strong labor market, but it does not tell the entire story. Measures of wages, labor force participation, and job availability provide additional context.

05 / Monetary policy

Interest Rates

Interest rates represent the cost of borrowing money or the return earned for lending or saving it. They influence financial decisions made by households, businesses, and governments.

The Federal Reserve

The Federal Reserve is the central bank of the United States. It conducts monetary policy with goals that include maximum employment and stable prices.

Its policy decisions can influence short-term interest rates and broader financial conditions.

How rates affect the economy

Higher interest rates can make borrowing more expensive for households and businesses. Lower rates can reduce borrowing costs, holding other factors constant.

Interest rates can influence spending, investment, saving, and inflation.

Why investors pay attention

Interest rates can affect bond prices, borrowing costs, business investment decisions, and the valuation of financial assets. Their effects depend on expectations and broader market conditions.

06 / International finance

Exchange Rates

An exchange rate measures the value of one currency relative to another. Exchange rates matter because countries, businesses, investors, and travelers frequently exchange currencies.

Currency appreciation

A currency appreciates when it increases in value relative to another currency. Holding other factors constant, this can make foreign products less expensive for domestic buyers.

Currency depreciation

A currency depreciates when it decreases in value relative to another currency. Holding other factors constant, imported products may become more expensive for domestic buyers.

Exchange rates connect economies

Currency movements can influence international trade, tourism, business revenue, and investment returns. Exchange rates are affected by many factors, including interest rates, inflation expectations, trade, and financial market activity.

07 / Putting the pieces together

Economic indicators work together

No single statistic explains everything happening in an economy. Economists examine several indicators together to build a more complete picture.

GDP and employment

Stronger production can be associated with increased demand for workers, although the relationship depends on productivity and other economic conditions.

Inflation and interest rates

Central banks monitor inflation when making monetary policy decisions. Interest rate changes can influence borrowing, spending, and economic activity.

Interest rates and exchange rates

Differences in interest rates and expectations about future policy can influence demand for currencies, although many other factors matter.

Think like an economist

When one indicator changes, ask what else might be happening. For example, rising GDP alongside falling unemployment can suggest improving economic conditions. But inflation, wages, productivity, and other measurements still provide important context.

09 / Reading statistics

How to interpret economic data

Finding a statistic is not enough. Understanding what it measures and how it was collected is essential to interpreting it correctly.

📅

Check the date

Identify the period covered by the statistic and when the information was published.

📏

Check the units

Determine whether the figure is a percentage, dollar amount, index, or annual growth rate.

🔍

Check the context

Compare the figure with earlier periods and consider revisions, seasonal adjustments, and other relevant information.

Use original sources whenever possible

Economic statistics can be revised as new information becomes available. When reporting a figure, identify its source, publication date, measurement period, and units. Avoid presenting an old statistic as if it were current.

10 / Essential vocabulary

Economic indicators glossary

Review these terms as you explore economic data.

GDP

The market value of final goods and services produced within a country's borders during a particular period.

Inflation

A general increase in prices over time.

Deflation

A sustained decline in the overall price level.

CPI

An index that measures changes in consumer prices over time.

Unemployment rate

The percentage of the labor force that is unemployed and actively seeking work.

Labor force

People who are employed or unemployed and actively looking for work.

Monetary policy

Actions taken by a central bank to influence money, credit, and financial conditions.

Exchange rate

The value of one currency expressed in another currency.
