PERSONAL FINANCE

Understanding Money.

Money influences nearly every part of modern life. Learn how people save, borrow, invest, and make financial decisions.

THE FOUNDATIONS

What is personal finance?

Personal finance is the management of an individual's or household's money. It involves decisions about earning, spending, saving, borrowing, and investing.

Understanding these concepts can help people evaluate financial choices and prepare for future goals.

01 / SAVING

Saving Money

Saving means setting aside money instead of spending all of it immediately. It can help people prepare for future expenses and financial goals.

Why Do People Save?

People save for many reasons, including unexpected expenses, education, major purchases, and long-term financial goals.

Savings can also provide flexibility when unexpected financial situations arise.

Simple Interest vs. Compound Interest

Simple interest is calculated using the original principal amount.

Compound interest allows previously earned interest to generate additional interest. Over time, this can cause savings to grow more quickly.

Key Concept

Compound interest is sometimes described as earning interest on interest. Time can have a significant influence on the growth of savings.

02 / BORROWING

Credit & Loans

Credit allows individuals and businesses to borrow money or make purchases with the agreement that payment will be made later.

What Is Credit?

Credit is the ability to borrow money or access goods and services with an agreement to pay later.

Lenders often consider a borrower's credit history and other financial information when evaluating an application.

How Do Loans Work?

A loan is money borrowed from a lender. The borrower generally agrees to repay the original amount, called the principal, over time.

Depending on the loan, interest and other charges may also apply.

Understanding Interest Rates

Interest is the cost of borrowing money. The interest rate helps determine how much borrowing costs over time.

The amount borrowed, interest rate, repayment schedule, and loan length all influence the total amount repaid.

Key Concept

A lower interest rate generally means borrowing costs less, assuming other loan terms are equal.

03 / INVESTING

Investing

Investing involves putting money into assets with the goal of potentially earning a return over time.

S

Stocks

A stock represents partial ownership in a company. Investors may benefit if the stock increases in value or if the company distributes dividends.

B

Bonds

A bond represents money lent to a government, company, or other organization. The issuer generally promises to repay the principal and make interest payments according to the bond's terms.

I

Index Funds

An index fund is designed to track a particular market index. It can hold many different investments, providing diversification.

Understanding Investment Risk

All investments involve some degree of risk. Their values can rise or fall depending on economic conditions, company performance, interest rates, and other factors.

Diversification involves spreading investments across different assets to reduce reliance on any single investment. It does not eliminate the possibility of losses.

Key Concept

Potential returns and investment risk are connected. Understanding both is an important part of financial education.

04 / VOCABULARY

Financial Terms to Know

Understanding financial vocabulary makes it easier to follow economic news and evaluate financial concepts.

Principal

The original amount of money borrowed or invested.

Interest

Money paid for borrowing or earned from certain savings and investments.

Inflation

A general increase in prices over time, which reduces the purchasing power of money.

Dividend

A distribution of part of a company's earnings to eligible shareholders.

Asset

Something of economic value that an individual or organization owns or controls.

Liability

A financial obligation or amount owed to another party.
